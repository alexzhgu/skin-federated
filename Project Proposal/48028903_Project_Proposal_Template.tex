Skin disease classification using deep learning has shown strong potential but training accurate models requires large, diverse datasets often distributed across healthcare institutions. Privacy regulations such as the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) prevent centralised data aggregation. Prior work has shown that even sharing model updates rather than raw data can leak sensitive patient information. Federated learning offers a framework for collaborative training across distributed nodes without centralising data, but standard federated averaging lacks convergence guarantees. This project evaluates federated learning approaches for multi-class skin disease classification in a simulated distributed healthcare environment. An dataset of approximately 20,000 images covering 30 skin disease classes will be partitioned across multiple nodes. FedAvg and FedProx will be implemented and compared against a centralised training baseline, evaluating classification accuracy, communication efficiency, and model behaviour under data that is not independently and identically distributed (non-IID).

\chapter{Introduction}
  Skin diseases are among the most common health conditions worldwide, and deep learning has demonstrated strong performance in automated skin disease classification from images. However, training models that generalise well requires large, diverse datasets that capture variation across patient populations, imaging conditions, and disease presentations. In practice, medical image data is distributed across hospitals, clinics, and research institutions, each holding relatively small local datasets. Privacy regulations including HIPAA and GDPR restrict the transfer and centralisation of patient data, creating a fundamental barrier to building robust classifiers through conventional centralised training.\\
  \\
  Federated learning addresses this by enabling multiple institutions to collaboratively train a shared model without exchanging raw data. Each participant trains on its local dataset and shares only model updates with a central server, which aggregates them into a global model. McMahan et al. introduced Federated Averaging (FedAvg), which reduces communication costs by performing multiple local training epochs before aggregation \cite{mcmahanCommunicationEfficientLearningDeep2023}. However, FedAvg lacks convergence guarantees when data across participants is non-IID, a condition that is common in healthcare settings where different institutions may see different patient demographics and disease distributions. FedProx extends FedAvg by introducing a proximal term that constrains local model updates, providing convergence guarantees under heterogeneous conditions \cite{liFederatedOptimizationHeterogeneous2020}. Furthermore, federated learning alone does not fully resolve privacy concerns — prior work has shown that shared model updates can leak sensitive information through inference attacks, and additional mechanisms such as secure aggregation and differential privacy may be needed \cite{kaissisSecurePrivacypreservingFederated2020}.\\
  \\
  This project evaluates federated learning for multi-class skin disease classification in a simulated distributed healthcare environment. A dataset of approximately 20,000 images covering 30 skin disease classes will be partitioned across multiple nodes. FedAvg and FedProx will be implemented and compared against a centralised training baseline, with evaluation focused on classification accuracy, communication efficiency, and model behaviour under non-IID data distributions.
  The remainder of this proposal is organised as follows. Chapter 2 provides background on federated learning, the challenges of statistical heterogeneity, and privacy considerations. Chapter 3 outlines the project plan, including methodology, milestones, and timeline.
